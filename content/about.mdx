# I design the trust between people and intelligent systems.

When an autonomous system acts, someone has to understand what it's doing, decide whether to believe it, and act on it, often under pressure. I design for that person. Here's how I came to this work.

<AboutPhoto />

## I started with a belief: technology should serve people

I began as an art director in advertising, then studied human-computer interaction at Carnegie Mellon. I came away believing software should take on the work so people can be more present with each other. I put that belief to work in biomedical informatics, designing systems people could talk to instead of type into. One of them, Field Guide, replaced paper surveys in an NIH-funded oral health study across 700 Appalachian households. It reached an 83% completion rate, and enrollment and dental visits both rose. At night, I taught myself to code.

## A family crisis changed my questions

Then a bicycle accident and a traumatic brain injury upended my family. Suddenly I was doing it all alone, and the overwhelm was constant. I needed to understand how a mind holds up under pressure, and how it heals. So I turned my attention to the system software is meant to serve: the human mind.

## Coaching taught me how trust is earned

After formal coursework in positive psychology, applied neuroscience, linguistics, and behavioral science, I became a credentialed coach. Uncertainty is uncomfortable, so people grab the first answer. My job was to earn enough trust that they'd stay with the question. One client spent session after session blaming a colleague, until she heard herself and realized she was describing her own behavior.

## Regulated fintech demanded clarity

In those same years, I was the sole product designer on a fintech product serving schools and borrowers. When people are paying, confusion makes them hesitate, so clarity was the work. The portals I designed cut payment abandonment by 84% and support calls by 62%.

## AI became the tool I'd been missing

When large language models arrived, I saw a way for software to pay close attention to people. Note-taking during sessions kept pulling my focus from clients, so I built an app that did the listening for me. It plotted the conversation live and highlighted the client's own language, making patterns like hers visible as they happened.

## Now I design AI that earns people's trust

I'm rebuilding Field Guide with AI: a voice interviewer that speaks with a familiar local accent, notices when participants tire, and catches answers that fall into the same pattern. I've also designed interfaces for drone fleets and data center operations, where people have to decide in real time whether to trust what a machine reports. That's the work I want to lead next: setting design direction for physical AI and mentoring the designers who build it.

> People decide whether to trust a system before they can say why. I design for that moment.
